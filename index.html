Rotating black holes and what surrounds them

Ho-Yun YuChih*

MSc in Theoretical Physics, Peking University, Beijing, China
BSc in Materials Science (Physics Track), Duke Kunshan University, Kunshan, China

I. INTRODUCTION

I want to understand spacetime, and black holes are where it is pushed to its extreme. In my master's research, I asked when magnetic reconnection in the ergoregion can actually take energy out of a Kerr black hole. Along the way I kept finding that the answer was decided less by the plasma than by the spacetime: the reconnection physics sets how fast the plasmoids are ejected, but whether their energy ever reaches infinity is fixed by the geodesic structure around the hole, and requiring that it does sets a spin floor of a = 0.8005. To see how much this depends on the theory of gravity itself, I also studied the process in a Kerr–Sen-like black hole of Einstein–bumblebee gravity, where Lorentz symmetry is spontaneously broken; I chose this metric because I am interested in both strings and fields, and it has the form of the Kerr–Sen solution of heterotic string theory. In this spacetime, a larger symmetry-breaking parameter or bumblebee charge moves the horizon inward and widens the ergoregion, which makes extraction easier; at fixed irreducible mass, extraction is most favourable when the spacetime is only marginally consistent with cosmic censorship. These results brought me back to the geometry, and I want to follow it in two directions. One is gravitational waves, where the geometry becomes dynamical and observable: how black holes and the fields around them shape the waves, and what departures from general relativity would leave in them. The other is the nature of spacetime: what becomes of geometry and gravity in a more complete theory, and whether they can be understood from something more fundamental. Holography was what first drew me to theoretical physics, and together with string theory it is where I want to pursue this question.

II. RESEARCH

My master's work is a series on energy extraction from rotating black holes by magnetic reconnection [1]–[4]. Each paper relaxes one assumption the earlier literature fixed for convenience, then measures how much the conclusion depended on it.

A. The plunging bulk plasma and orientation angle

Plasma plunging inside the ISCO favours plasmoids ejected at a positive angle to the flow, which widens the region where the local energy condition holds. [1]

B. Parameters in reconnection models

Letting the reconnection-model parameters vary shows that the Sweet–Parker limit overestimates how often an accretion flow can extract energy. [2]

C. Bumblebee gravity

In a Kerr–Sen-like black hole of Einstein–bumblebee gravity, a larger rate of Lorentz symmetry breaking or a larger bumblebee charge broadens the regions where energy extraction succeeds and moves them inward; at fixed irreducible mass, extraction is most favourable when the spacetime is marginally consistent with cosmic censorship. [3]

D. Destinies of plasmoids from reconnection in the ergoregion

Requiring the energetic plasmoid to actually escape sets a spin floor of a = 0.8005: below it, reconnection cannot extract energy at any magnetization. This suggests that spin-down by successive reconnections stops near this value. [4]
